## The question

To understand the Aural Life World of an Ethnic group or a Community. Through which we can comprehend their lived experiences (embedded in material & non material structures) of Aural Landscapes & make meaning of sounds which may not exist in the real world but have meanings for the group or the community.

The study looked at three levels of sound:

1. Objective sound, ambient: anyone has access to it.
2. Objective sound, subjective in nature: limited access.
3. Sound that doesn't exist at all, but has meaning for the group.

## My role

I chose a group whose work involves sound: four artisans at Manek Chowk who predominantly make silver jewellery for the tribal markets of Madhya Pradesh. I took up the role of a student who is there to learn the craft, worked as a part of the group and tried to gain access. I kept a check on how I proceeded so I would not threaten any of their beliefs or notions, as I was the only woman in a male work environment.

## Methods

Ethnography is subjective in nature: it requires the researcher to participate directly in the setting, and it is open to interpretation because units of measurement are not decided. I took a phenomenological approach, studying the group's lived experience in terms of the meanings they had constructed of it. The method moved from understanding my own "frame" (world view), to identifying the group and my role, to participant observations and long interviews, to open coding, synthesis and analysis.

Plans changed in the field. The owner, the "Seth", was the person in power and denied any interviews with the group. I showed him the questionnaire to convince him, persuaded him to give his own long interview, and gained enough trust that he allowed long interviews with the karigars (workers).

I recorded the first level of sound (ambient), open-coded the participant observations, and coded the sounds and their relations, both literal and metaphorical. Through synthesis I interpreted the second and third levels of sound, and represented the findings in physical artefacts.

## Who I worked with

The four members of the group, with a clear power dynamic: the Seth (owner, skilled worker), the Seth's old friend (skilled worker), the youngest (apprentice) and the oldest (manual labour worker).

Acknowledgements: Dr. Anand Sukumaran and Dr. Jignesh Khakhar. Thanks: Tanuja Mishra and Mayukhini Pande.

## Key insights

> Noise and silence emerged as the most important categories. Noise covered the source of the noise and filtering it out, for example with the radio. Silence covered the power equation, seamlessness and the void of sounds.

> In a day, the sound the group generated depended highly on the power equation shared within the group.

## Impact

- **Level 1, visual and sound artefact:** replicates one of the ambient sounds that emerged in the category "Playing", with "Pleasing" as the subcategory.
- **Level 3, visual artefact of sounds that do not exist:** a coiled silver spring that maps sound visually. Its shape comes from the group's daily work, the stage of cutting a spring to make hand-linked chains. Literal mapping: more rings show more work and noise, and voids show breaks and silence. Metaphorical mapping: the number and radius of the rings show the power behind the sound each person generated.
- **Issues I worked through:** gender, beliefs, the power structure of the group, gaining trust and access, spoken language, difficulty of engagement and the roles available to me.
- **Learnings:** my "world view" influences the data; tackling inhibitions, biases and power structures; qualitative data through reflection in action and on action.
- **Recordings:** Aural Landscape: Ambient Sounds, and Sound Artefact: Inspiration for the physical artefact (SoundCloud, above).
- **Report:** [Understanding Aural Life Worlds](https://www.scribd.com/doc/76078080/Understanding-Aural-Life-Worlds) (Scribd), the complete documentation of the project.
